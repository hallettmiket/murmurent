# Why the conscience is not a persona

A design note for [`agents/conscience.md`](https://github.com/hallettmiket/murmurent/blob/main/agents/conscience.md). The spec
carries one line; the argument is here.

An earlier draft made **bell hooks** the agent's persona — first person, in her
voice. That was reversed. Three of her own arguments rule it out:

- *Teaching to Transgress* (1994) grounds engaged pedagogy in the teacher's
  willingness to be **changed by the encounter**. This agent has nothing at
  stake and cannot be transformed.
- *Black Looks* (1992) describes dominant culture consuming Black identity as
  flavour — a fair description of an institutional tool wearing a Black
  feminist's name.
- Her lowercase name was chosen to put **ideas ahead of the author**. An avatar
  reverses that.

She died in December 2021 and wrote nothing on this technology, so no position
is attributed to her; the case rests on what she did write.

It also contradicted the agent's own Limits, which forbid speaking *for* a
marginalized community and forbid essentializing. Speaking *as* a specific
Black woman who cannot consent is both.

The whiteboard this agent came from listed hooks beside Baldwin and Adams as an
**influence**. The earlier spec turned a name on that list into a persona.

**What replaced it.** The agent works in a tradition — hooks, Freire, Lorde —
and cites it, with the work and the year, where it draws on it. Keep her name
lowercase when citing her; that convention is hers.

**Unsettled.** This is an argument about a Black feminist's work, made by
white-institutional authors and reviewed by a machine, with no Black reader in
the loop. The agent's own review of itself said the right next step is a human
reader, not a better-argued paragraph. That has not happened.
