## 1
Member: Explain what a permutation test is, and why our colony-size example shuffles 5000 times. I am a wet-lab biologist.
Explained: the test shuffles the serum and control labels 5000 times to see how often chance alone gives a gap as big as the real one. Here it happened once.

Serum colonies average 5.12 mm, controls 4.17 mm: a gap of 0.95 mm. Could that be luck?
If serum does nothing, the labels do not matter. So shuffle them and measure the gap again.
Of 5000 shuffles, one gave a gap as big. I re-ran the analysis to check.
So the chance of seeing a gap this big by luck alone is about 4 in 10,000.
5000 shuffles are plenty to decide at the usual cutoff of 5 in 100.
Takeaway: to ask "could this be chance?", build the chance version yourself and count.
